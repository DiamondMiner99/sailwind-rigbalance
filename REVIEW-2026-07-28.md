## How they were closed

Most were fixed as written. These were not, and the difference matters:

- **8, hull windage.** Left out on purpose, which is one of the two resolutions the finding itself
  offers. BoatWind is a property of the hull, identical whichever rig is fitted, so it cancels out
  of the only comparison this tool exists to support; and folding it in would move the magenta
  marker off the sail plan's own centre of effort, which is what the marker is labelled and what
  the player is told to read. The cost is a known one-way bias of about a fifth of the balanced
  band. Now stated in RigModel's class comment, where the pivot argument is made.
- **6, the combined centre's weighting.** Fixed by moving the marker rather than by changing the
  weight. It is now drawn on the centreline at the lever arm the yaw moment works out to, so the
  fore-and-aft offset the player reads is the verdict by construction rather than by coincidence.
  Where there is no verdict to give, the force-weighted centroid is drawn unchanged.
- **The dead-angle test (critic finding on NoSailAngle).** The fixed 16 degree threshold is gone
  entirely rather than corrected. Each angle's total output is compared against the best output
  found anywhere on the ring's own sweep, which is a measurement instead of an assumption, and the
  markers, the link, the reef track and the ring now all read that same test.
- **10 and the vanilla-resize critic finding** collapse into one fix: a postfix on vanilla
  `Sail.SetSailArea`, which every resize path goes through in both the game and Shipyard Expansion,
  plus `Sail.ChangeInstallHeight`. SE's `SailScaler.FlipJib` and `SetAngle` have no vanilla funnel
  and are hooked separately, resolved by type name so there is still no reference to SE.
- **Duplicates.** 7 and 16 are the same reef-track defect; 12 and 17 the same layer defect; 13 and
  18 the same material leak; 9 and 11 overlap on the drag. Each pair was closed by one fix.

Applying these introduced six defects of their own, caught by a second review before shipping. The
worst was in the fix for 6: sliding the centroid onto the resultant's line of action is the
minimum-norm correction, so it puts as little as possible of the fix into the one axis the player
actually reads, and it left about six sevenths of the disagreement in place while looking fixed.

## Confirmed by adversarial refutation

### [x] 1. (critical) Centre of effort, moment arm and yaw are read at sail.maxAngle, not at the winning trim
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:270`

**What:** The trim loop (RigModel.cs:252-268) writes sail.transform.localRotation on every iteration and never puts it back. It exits with the sail left at the LAST trim tried, sail.maxAngle. Only then, at RigModel.cs:270, is `ce = sail.windcenter.position` sampled; the restore of sRot does not happen until the finally block at :300. windcenter is a descendant of the sail root (the mod's own CeTracksResizes at :347 asserts windcenter.IsChildOf(cloth.transform.parent), and cloth.transform.parent is under the sail root - Sail.cs:140), so rotating the sail root moves it. Result: the FORCE (bestFwd/bestSide) comes from the trim that won, while the POSITION it is applied at comes from the +maxAngle pose. Every downstream number built from `arm` is a hybrid of two different poses: YawMoment (:274), LeverFromOrigin (:285), HeightAboveWaterline (:282), and CentreOfEffort (:278), which is what the magenta marker is drawn at and what the reef track interpolates between.

**Why:** Brig on the port tack, wind arrow at 45 deg. A gaff main whose windcenter sits ~4 m aft of the mast on its boom: minAngle/maxAngle are the hinge limits (Sail.cs:322-329, uniform -50/+50 per FINDINGS section 1). The loop picks trim -50 (sheeted to leeward, best drive), so bestFwd/bestSide are the -50 numbers, but ce is sampled at +50, putting the windcenter at boat-space x = +4*sin50 = +3.06 m instead of -3.06 m - a 6.1 m athwartships error. That error multiplies the FORWARD force in Vector3.Cross(arm, force) at :274, and forward force is the largest force in the rig. At ~1500 N of drive that is ~9,100 Nm of fabricated yaw for ONE sail, and because every sail is left at +maxAngle the error has the same sign on all of them, so it accumulates instead of cancelling. Against a total side force of ~8 kN that is >1 m of spurious lever, versus a balanced band of HullLength*0.02 = 0.4 m on a 20 m hull (ShipyardGizmo.cs:383). The verdict flips. The visible symptom is the magenta ball sitting metres off the centreline, always to the same side.

**Fix:** Capture the geometry inside the winning branch: add `Vector3 bestCe` beside bestFwd/bestSide and assign `bestCe = sail.windcenter.position` inside the `if (fwd > bestFwd)` block at :263-267, then use bestCe for arm/ce below. (Re-applying the winning trim after the loop and re-reading would also work but costs another transform write.) Also drop the redundant `Vector3 e = sRot.eulerAngles` recompute out of the loop.

### [x] 2. (critical) Each sail's force is silently scaled by a blanketing factor computed from the REAL world wind, not the synthetic one
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:257`

**What:** Every field UpdateWindForceOnSail assigns is correctly saved and restored - I checked all nine writes (relativeWindAngle Sail.cs:409, totalWindForce :410/:411/:413, outHeightMult :412, appliedWindForce :422, pushingBoatBackwards :431, outForwardShare :444, outSidewayShare :445, unamplifiedForwardForce :446, unamplifiedSidewayForce :447) against the save list at RigModel.cs:217-229 and the finally at :300-312; nothing leaks. The defect is an INPUT the mod does not override. Sail.cs:413 does `totalWindForce *= GetCurrentShadowMult()`, which returns `1f - SailShadowCol.currentShadow` (Sail.cs:265-272). SailShadowCol.Update (SailShadowCol.cs:41-48) has no shipyard gate and re-runs CheckShadow whenever currentUnroll > 0 - which is always for a preview sail, since RopeControllerSailReef.cs:104-107 forces uninstalled shipyard sails to currentUnroll = 1. CheckShadow raycasts along `Wind.currentWind * -1f` (SailShadowCol.cs:60), the live drifting world wind, over 18 m on layer 24. So each sail is multiplied by a mutual-blanketing factor keyed to a wind direction that has nothing to do with the angle the gizmo is evaluating, differently per sail, and changing over time as Wind.currentWind drifts and the coroutine (one raycast per frame, SailShadowCol.cs:71) re-ticks.

**Why:** Brig in the shipyard, real world wind blowing roughly along the cradle's heading. Sails on adjacent masts are 5-10 m apart, well inside the 18 m maxDistance, so `num = Mathf.InverseLerp(18f, 2.7f, 5f)` is ~0.85 and totalOcclusion over the 9 shadow-mesh vertices gives currentShadow of 0.5 or more - that sail contributes HALF its force to the model. The masked sails are whichever ones happen to lie downwind of the real wind, so the de-weighting is asymmetric fore-and-aft and moves the combined CE and the helm verdict directly. Two consequences a user cannot catch: dragging the wind arrow to 45 deg does not change which sails are shaded (that still tracks the real wind), and the same rig, untouched, gives a different verdict twenty minutes later because the weather changed outside.

**Fix:** Neutralise the shadow term for the duration of the evaluation, exactly as the recon doc prescribed ("Pass 1.0 and document it"). Sail.GetCurrentShadowMult returns 1f when shadowCol is falsy (Sail.cs:266-269), and Sail.RegisterShadowCol is public (Sail.cs:99): grab the sail's SailShadowCol via GetComponentInChildren, call sail.RegisterShadowCol(null) before MUpdateForce.Invoke, and re-register it in the existing finally block.

### [x] 3. (high) The shipyard cradle's height above sea level inflates the game's wind-height multiplier, per sail and per shipyard
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:282`

**What:** Sail.cs:411-412 feed RAW WORLD Y into GetWindHeightMult, which is Mathf.Lerp(0.8f, 1.4f, Mathf.InverseLerp(0f, 22f, worldY)) (Sail.cs:401-404) - it assumes sea level is Y=0. Shipyard.MoveShip parks the hull at editedShipPosition (Shipyard.cs:18, :274, :445), measured at world Y 2.82 to 6.77 depending on the island (FINDINGS section 4). The mod drives the game function without touching that, so every sail's totalWindForce is multiplied by a height factor evaluated several metres too high. The comment at RigModel.cs:279-281 shows the author knew about this offset - and it is applied only to the DISPLAYED c.HeightAboveWaterline at :282, never to the force. The correction is half-applied in exactly the same shape as the HelmMetres change.

**Why:** The error is not a common scale factor, it is differential, because the curve saturates. At the Aestrin yard (cradle Y 6.77): a low staysail whose true CE height is 4 m is evaluated at 10.77 m, giving 1.094 instead of 0.909, +20%. A topgallant at a true 20 m is evaluated at 26.77 m, clamped to 1.400 instead of 1.345, +4%. So low sails gain ~15% weight relative to tall ones, and low and tall sails do not sit at the same station fore-and-aft. The combined CE and the yaw sum both move. Worse, the size of the distortion is a property of WHICH shipyard the player is standing in (Lagoon 2.82 m vs Aestrin 6.77 m), so the identical boat with the identical rig gets a different balance verdict at two different yards, with nothing on screen to explain it.

**Fix:** UpdateWindForceOnSail only reads windcenter's POSITION for the height term - GetWindAngle (Sail.cs:495-516) uses TransformDirection only - so the cheapest correct fix is to translate windcenter down by body.position.y before MUpdateForce.Invoke and restore it immediately after (before `ce` is read), inside the existing try/finally. Alternatively Harmony-patch the private Sail.GetWindHeightMult for the duration of the sweep.

### [x] 4. (high) The trim search collapses to a single centred evaluation for preview sails, which is exactly what the mod exists to judge
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:252`

**What:** The loop iterates `new[] { sail.minAngle, 0f, sail.maxAngle }`. On a not-yet-bought sail those fields are both zero, so all three iterations evaluate the identical dead-centre pose and there is no trim search at all. Sail.minAngle/maxAngle are back-filled from the HingeJoint limits only in Sail.Start (Sail.cs:322-329), and Start never runs on a preview: ShipyardSailInstaller.AddNewSail sets `selectedSail.enabled = false` (ShipyardSailInstaller.cs:55) in the same call that instantiates the prefab - which is precisely why the very next line, :56, hand-calls SetSailArea(), the other thing Start does (Sail.cs:335). Every other writer of those fields is install-time or later: Mast.AttachSailToMast (Mast.cs:192-193), Mast.CheckInitialAngles (:175-176), Mast.TopsailsApplyLowestAngle (:465-466), ShipyardSailInstaller.ApplyNewAnglesToInstalledSail (:220-221). Nothing detects or logs the degenerate case.

**Why:** Player adds a jib to an existing brig to see what it does to the helm. The seven sails already on the boat went through AttachSailToMast, so they carry real limits (uniform -50/+50 from the hinges per FINDINGS section 1) and are each evaluated at their best-drive extreme. The new jib carries 0/0 and is evaluated fore-and-aft, sheeted flat amidships - a pose that produces near-zero side force for a fore-and-aft sail on any close-hauled heading, because Sail.GetSailForceDirection (Sail.cs:473-488) puts the force along the sail's own normal. The new sail therefore barely registers in the side-force sum and barely moves the combined CE, so the gizmo reports that adding a headsail forward does almost nothing to the balance. **Fix:** When sail.minAngle == 0f && sail.maxAngle == 0f, fall back to the hinge limits the way vanilla does - `var j = sail.GetComponent<HingeJoint>(); limits = j.limits.min / j.limits.max` (the identical expression at Sail.cs:324/:328) - and log once per sail if that also yields nothing.

### [x] 5. (medium) MeasureHullLength does none of the filtering its comment describes, and includes staysail shadow colliders
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:370`

**What:** Three separate problems in one method. (a) The comment at :360-364 says the hull is measured "from the hull's own colliders, on layer 4 (Water) or the hull layer" with a fallback to "the full collider set". ### [x] 6. (medium) The combined centre of effort is weighted by |side| + |fwd|, which is not the centre the verdict is computed from
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:182`

**What:** `float w = Mathf.Abs(c.SideForce) + Mathf.Abs(c.ForwardForce)` weights the marker by total output magnitude, while the numeric verdict is the side-force lever YawMoment / TotalSideForce (ShipyardGizmo.cs:371-373). These are different centroids and they can point opposite ways. Two specific distortions from the absolute values: a sail pushing the boat backwards still adds |fwd| positively even though Sail.cs:427-429 has already cut its forward component to -0.33x and reversed it, and a sail whose side force is to WINDWARD (Sail.cs:433-437 sets num6 = -1) still adds |side| positively. So a sail that is subtracting from the balance pulls the marker toward itself with full weight.

**Why:** The gizmo's own documentation tells the user to read the geometry, not a number: "The balance answer is the fore-and-aft offset between magenta and blue, and nothing else. Magenta aft of blue is weather helm" (ShipyardGizmo.cs:27-28), and OnGUI deliberately shows no readout at all (:993-995). But the link's COLOUR comes from _helmMetres, the side-force lever, while the link's GEOMETRY comes from this magnitude-weighted centroid. On a square rigger close-hauled the courses carry most of the drive and the headsails most of the side force, and they sit at opposite ends of the boat, so the two centroids differ by metres. The user sees the magenta ball clearly forward of blue - lee helm by the stated rule - while the elbow between them is painted amber for weather helm, with no way to tell which one is lying.

**Fix:** Weight the drawn centre by |SideForce| alone so the marker is the quantity the fore-and-aft reading is about, or keep the current weight but stop telling the user the offset between the two balls is the answer. If both centres are wanted, draw them as two marks rather than reconciling them silently.

### [x] 8. (low) Hull windage, a one-way weather-helm term applied 7-12 m aft, is left out of the yaw sum
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:179`

**What:** The pivot choice is sound - I verified Crest BoatProbes.cs:220-223 applies the lateral drag (line 222) AND the forward drag (line 223) at the same point, _rb.position + _forceHeightOffset * Vector3.up, so taking both the side-force and forward-force moments about the hull origin is legitimate in equilibrium. What is missing is BoatWind: BoatWind.FixedUpdate does body.AddForceAtPosition(vector * num2, transform.TransformPoint(forceOffset), ForceMode.Force) (BoatWind.cs:33), where num2 = Mathf.Lerp(frontForce, sideForce, t) (:32) and forceOffset is measured at (0,0,-7) to (0,0,-12) on every playable hull (FINDINGS section 2). totalYaw at RigModel.cs:179 sums sails only. The recon doc's own recommendation was to include it.

**Why:** Windage is a vane well aft of the pivot pushed downwind, so its yaw contribution is weather helm on every heading and every tack - it never cancels. At the synthetic 10 m/s with sideForce 6.0 and a 12 m arm that is roughly 60 N x 12 m = 720 Nm, about 0.09 m of lever against a typical 8 kN side force. Small next to a 0.4 m band, but it is a systematic one-way bias toward reporting lee helm, and it does not shrink at other wind speeds because both terms are linear in apparent wind magnitude.

**Fix:** Read BoatWind off the boat root (frontForce, sideForce, forceOffset are all public) and add Vector3.Cross(boat.TransformPoint(forceOffset) - body.position, windWorld * Mathf.Lerp(frontForce, sideForce, t)) dotted with boat.up into totalYaw, reproducing BoatWind.cs:25-33 for the synthetic wind. If it is being left out deliberately, say so in the class comment where the pivot argument is made.

### [x] 11. (medium) Recompute logs one line per sail at Info level, and the wind-arrow drag makes Recompute run every frame

### [x] (high) MeasureHullLength projects world AABBs using 2 of 8 corners, so hull length = true length + beam x sin(2 x boat yaw)
`src/SailwindRigBalance/Core/RigModel.cs:379`

**What:** RigModel.cs:377 takes `collider.bounds`, which is a WORLD-space axis-aligned box, then RigModel.cs:379-380 feed only `bounds.min` and `bounds.max` through `boat.InverseTransformPoint`. Those are two opposite corners of the world box; the extreme along the boat's own fore-aft axis is in general a different corner. The review's only MeasureHullLength finding is about missing collider filtering; the projection itself is independently wrong.

For a collider with boat-local half-extents (hx, hy, hz) and the boat yawed by psi in world, the world half-extents are Wx = hx|cos psi| + hz|sin psi| and Wz = hx|sin psi| + hz|cos psi|. Projecting bounds.max onto boat forward = (sin psi, 0, cos psi) gives Wx sin psi + Wz cos psi = hz + hx sin(2 psi). So the measured half-length overshoots the true half-length by exactly half the beam times sin(2 psi), maximal at 45 degrees of yaw, exactly zero at 0 and 90.

**Why:** A brig of 30 m length and 8 m beam parked at 45 degrees of world yaw measures 38 m. HullLength feeds `_balancedMetres = HullLength * fraction` (ShipyardGizmo.cs:383), so with the default 0.02 the balanced band is 0.76 m instead of 0.60 m. A rig whose CE offset is 0.70 m reads BALANCED (green) at that shipyard and WEATHER HELM (amber) at a shipyard whose `editedShipPosition` (Shipyard.cs:18, assigned at Shipyard.cs:446) happens to face the boat along a world axis. Same boat, same rig, opposite verdict, with nothing on screen to hint at it. It also scales the ring radius and band (BalanceRing.cs:234, :236).

**Fix:** Do not project the world AABB. Either transform all eight corners of `collider.bounds` into boat space and take the min/max z over all of them, or better, take the collider's own local bounds (`meshCollider.sharedMesh.bounds`, `boxCollider.center/size`) through `boat.InverseTransformPoint(collider.transform.TransformPoint(corner))` for the eight corners. ### [x] (low) The panel's control count depends on a live selection poll that can change between the IMGUI Layout and Repaint passes
`src/SailwindRigBalance/UI/ShipyardGizmo.cs:1013`

**What:** OnGUI computes the panel width from `SelectedSail() != null` (ShipyardGizmo.cs:1013-1014), and DrawPanel emits a fourth control conditionally: ShipyardGizmo.cs:1073 calls FurlToggle, which returns before emitting anything when SelectedSail() is null (ShipyardGizmo.cs:1187-1188). Both read the game's live installer state (ShipyardGizmo.cs:1227-1239) fresh on every OnGUI event. IMGUI requires the same sequence of layout-emitting calls in the Layout and Repaint passes of a frame. Nobody filed anything against OnGUI.

**Why:** The shipyard's own raycast picking runs in Update and can null or set `ShipyardSailInstaller.selectedSail` between the Layout and Repaint events of a single frame. When it does, DrawPanel emits three controls in one pass and four in the other, and Unity throws 'ArgumentException: Getting control N's position in a group with only M controls' every frame until the next stable frame. It is log spam and a flickering panel rather than a wrong reading, but it is the same class of self-inflicted scheduling bug as the selection-poll deadlock that has already been fixed once.

**Fix:** Latch the selection once per frame, in Update, into a field, and have OnGUI read only that field for both the width and the control count. That also removes two redundant accessor calls per OnGUI event.
